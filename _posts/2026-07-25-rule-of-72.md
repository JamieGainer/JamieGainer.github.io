I was thinking about quantifying how prices have changed lately, 
and it was making me think of "the rule of 72", 
which is a rough heuristic for how long it takes to double 
your money at $p\%$ interest.  (The answer is $72 / p$).

To understand why this is true we solve for $n$ in the equation
$$
(1 + p/100)^n = 2.
$$

Taking the (natural) log of both sides, we obtain
$$
n (\log{1 + p/100}) = \log{2}.
$$

So if we take the frist term in the Taylor series for $\log{1 + x}$, 
we obtain 
$$
np/100 = \log{2},
$$
So $n = 69.31/p$.  But you'll notice that $69.31 \neq 72$.  
So the issue here is for the realistic small percentages where one might want to use
an approximation like this, the next term in the Taylor series will "correct" 
the value in the numberator to be closer to $72$.
